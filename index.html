Pixel Pantry

A recipe app that turns whatever is in your fridge into a plan.

  • React
  • Node
  • Postgres

What it is

A web app where you type in the ingredients you have and get back recipes ranked by how few extra items you would need to buy.

Motivation

I kept opening the fridge with no idea what to cook, and every recipe app assumed I already had a full pantry.

Purpose

To practice building a full product end to end: a real backend, a matching algorithm, and a UI people would actually enjoy using.

Challenges

Matching loose ingredient names (e.g. “tomato” vs “cherry tomatoes”) was harder than expected and needed a small normalization layer.

What I learned

Got comfortable designing a schema for messy, user-generated ingredient data, and learned how much UX work goes into “good enough” search.

Routewise

A trip planner that optimizes multi-stop road trips for scenery, not just speed.

  • Vue
  • Mapbox
  • Python

What it is

A route planner where you drop pins for places you want to see, and it reorders and suggests the drive that balances time against detour-worthy stops.

Motivation

Planning a road trip with friends always turned into a spreadsheet fight over the “best” order to visit places.

Purpose

To explore routing algorithms and mapping APIs, and to ship something my friends would actually use on our next trip.

Challenges

Balancing “shortest route” against “most interesting route” needed a scoring system that took a few iterations to feel right.

What I learned

Learned how to work with real-world map data and constraint-based optimization, and how to communicate trade-offs simply in the UI.

Loopline

A tiny browser tool for sketching short audio loops without opening a DAW.

  • TypeScript
  • Web Audio API

What it is

A minimal step-sequencer that runs entirely in the browser, for quickly sketching a beat idea without launching a full production app.

Motivation

I wanted to capture musical ideas the moment they happened, but opening a heavy DAW always killed the momentum.

Purpose

To get properly comfortable with the Web Audio API and timing-accurate scheduling in JavaScript.

Challenges

Keeping playback perfectly on-beat across tabs and devices took a scheduling rewrite using a look-ahead timer pattern.

What I learned

Learned how audio scheduling actually works under the hood, and why naive setTimeout-based sequencers drift out of sync.
